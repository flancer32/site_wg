## Offer Status

The MCP Integration Pilot is the primary provisional productized-engineering direction for near-term cash flow.

It is a bounded offer hypothesis, not a standardized package or claim of repeatable prior delivery. The offer exists at product-context level because it presents a focused buyer outcome close to Alex's current technical direction while leaving evidence-dependent packaging decisions explicit.

## Buyer Problem And Outcome

The provisional buyer has an existing digital service, application, or API that an authorized AI agent should use, but the service does not yet expose the required operations through a controlled MCP boundary.

The provisional outcome is:

```text
existing customer service or API
  -> controlled MCP access
  -> selected tools available to an authorized AI agent
  -> one or more agreed workflows verified
```

The pilot should begin from an existing customer resource and a bounded useful workflow. It must not begin from a generic promise to invent an arbitrary AI system.

## Candidate Scope

A pilot may include an agreed subset of:

- analysis of the existing API or service boundary;
- an MCP server exposing selected operations;
- authentication and authorization handling appropriate to the agreed context;
- explicit tool and resource scoping;
- connection to ChatGPT, Codex, Claude, or another compatible agent environment;
- deployment and configuration;
- logging or operational visibility appropriate to the pilot;
- verification of the selected workflow;
- concise delivery and boundary documentation.

The customer system, operations, agent environment, deployment model, and acceptance evidence must be agreed before work begins. This list describes possible ingredients, not a fixed package.

## Buyer Value Hypothesis

The pilot may be valuable when it allows an existing service to participate safely and usefully in real agent-assisted work without rebuilding that service or granting broader authority than the workflow requires.

Alex's relevant leverage may include experience with MCP-oriented PDE and Desk development, TeqFW, ADSM, API integration, deployment, and long-lived systems. These foundations justify focused exploration; they do not prove that every customer system can be integrated quickly or securely.

## Authority And Trust Boundary

Before delivery, Alex and the customer must explicitly agree:

- the service, environment, data, and operations in scope;
- credential ownership and administrative access;
- authentication and authorization responsibilities;
- agent identity and permitted tools or resources;
- deployment ownership and operating responsibility;
- logging, retention, and inspection expectations;
- acceptance evidence and failure behavior at product level;
- termination, access revocation, and material handoff boundaries;
- confidentiality and whether any result may become public evidence or reusable capability.

The pilot must expose only the authority needed for the agreed workflow. It must not imply that using MCP makes an integration secure, compliant, reliable, or production-ready by default.

## Commercial Boundary

The commercial form is initially:

- a bounded paid pilot;
- scope and price agreed before work begins;
- one existing service or API boundary unless a different scope is explicit;
- selected operations and workflows rather than unlimited tool exposure;
- manual qualification, delivery, and review;
- explicit separation of pilot acceptance from later production operation or support.

No fixed public price, delivery time, SLA, enterprise-security guarantee, supported-service list, hosting commitment, or maintenance package is established. A pilot may reveal that the source system or authority boundary is unsuitable; discovery work and stopping conditions must be addressed in the agreement rather than hidden behind a delivery promise.

## Exclusions

Unless separately agreed, the pilot excludes:

- invention or replacement of the customer's core service;
- a general AI strategy or arbitrary automation programme;
- autonomous authority beyond selected operations;
- unlimited APIs, tools, agent environments, users, or workflows;
- unbounded data migration or cleanup;
- continuous hosting, monitoring, support, or incident response;
- legal, privacy, security, compliance, availability, performance, or scale guarantees;
- automatic transfer of reusable Wired Geese technology, source rights, or cognitive context;
- reuse of customer-confidential code, data, or knowledge.

## Validation Questions

Early paid pilots must discover:

- which payer problem is urgent and valuable enough to fund integration;
- which source services and APIs recur;
- which operations and agent workflows produce observable value;
- which authentication, authorization, and deployment patterns repeat;
- what delivery effort, risk, and customer coordination are typical;
- which components or knowledge can lawfully and usefully be reused;
- whether later support or recurring operation produces continuing value;
- whether the work should remain engineering, become a repeatable integration, or become an independent product.

One successful pilot proves only that its bounded outcome was achieved under its stated conditions. Standard scope, pricing, timing, support, and recurring models require repeated evidence.

## Strategic Fit

The pilot is preferred over unrelated custom work when it can produce immediate revenue, current agent-development evidence, reusable integration capability, and payer-problem knowledge. It may strengthen PDE, Desks, TeqFW, ADSM, TeqCMS, or related infrastructure when rights and technical fit allow.

The customer outcome remains primary. Internal strategic reuse never authorizes a weaker delivery or use of customer material beyond agreement.

## Commercial Next Step

The next step is a direct qualification conversation about the existing service, desired agent workflow, API readiness, operations and data in scope, authority boundary, deployment responsibility, acceptance evidence, confidentiality, and stopping conditions.

Final offer name, public copy, standard scope, price, delivery estimate, contract, form, route, supported matrix, and ongoing-support model remain open until evidence supports them.

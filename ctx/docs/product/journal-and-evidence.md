## Purpose

Define Journal/Events as the public evidence stream of the Wired Geese model and establish claim-specific evidence rules for every product and commercial surface.

`software-estate.md` is the canonical repository inventory and defines its non-commercial evidence limits. Repository evidence is a factual current-practice input to this model; it is never a substitute for a claim-specific Event or external/commercial evidence.

The Journal documents the attempt to build useful software and an economically sustainable software-making practice. It does not announce success before evidence exists.

## Journal As A First-Class Surface

Journal is one of the site's primary strategic surfaces because it makes development and commercial learning observable over time. It must be intentionally discoverable from primary navigation or an equivalently prominent persistent surface, Home recent activity, relevant system and offer pages, and contextual links.

Journal is not merely:

- a conventional blog;
- a release feed;
- an archive of dated writing;
- search-acquisition support;
- a sequence of success stories.

It is the cumulative public record of what Alex and the agents attempt, build, test, deploy, learn, sell, reuse, change, reject, or discontinue.

Journal prominence does not mean publishing volume. Fewer technically or economically meaningful events are preferable to a high-volume activity feed. Small connected events become valuable when they expose a durable sequence from intent through implementation and external evidence, but routine output must not be published merely to maintain cadence.

## Journal And Event Model

A **Journal** is the ordered, discoverable body of current evidence.

An **Event** is a dated record of a meaningful state change, observation, decision, or result. Examples include:

- selecting a problem or hypothesis;
- authorizing an agent task and accepting or rejecting its result;
- implementing or deploying a capability;
- recording a failure or abandoned direction;
- receiving external use or feedback;
- receiving a qualified commercial lead or rejecting an offer hypothesis;
- beginning or completing a paid pilot or integration;
- observing repeated delivery, reuse, or a customer result;
- receiving a recurring payment where disclosure is appropriate;
- changing a product assumption, offer boundary, or status;
- discontinuing a system or experiment.

An event may be short. A longer Journal entry may interpret several related events. The public labels `Journal`, `Events`, or another idiomatic presentation may evolve, but the semantic requirement is an inspectable chronology rather than generic blogging.

## Cumulative Proof Threads

Events should form explicit threads when they concern the same system, offer, hypothesis, or customer problem. A reader should be able to follow, where disclosure is safe:

```text
intent or problem
  -> human decision and authorized agent work
  -> implementation or experiment
  -> verification or failure
  -> deployment or external use
  -> payment or rejection
  -> reuse or boundary change
  -> current status and next question
```

Not every thread reaches every step. A stopped thread must retain its truthful ending rather than disappear from the narrative. The absence of paid or recurring evidence must remain visible when that absence is material to the claim.

## Event Content Responsibilities

A useful event identifies enough of the following to establish meaning:

- what changed or was learned;
- why the work or decision mattered;
- Alex's consequential choice or acceptance responsibility;
- how AI agents participated when that participation is relevant to the Wired Geese hypothesis;
- the affected system, capability, offer, or hypothesis;
- the result, including failure or uncertainty;
- inspectable supporting evidence where disclosure permits;
- the claim the event can support and the claims it cannot support;
- the relationship to earlier or later events;
- the resulting status, next question, or discontinuation.

This is a semantic checklist, not a mandatory public template. Events must remain readable and proportionate. Routine activity that changes no meaningful state need not be published merely to make the stream look busy.

## Relationship To Other Content

- **Journal/Events** record change, chronology, experiments, and accumulating evidence.
- **Library or durable articles** explain concepts intended to remain useful apart from a date or current project state.
- **System and product pages** state the current accepted meaning, maturity, capability, and commercial boundary.
- **Offer pages** state the current bounded buyer outcome and engagement boundary.
- **Projects and historical pages** preserve completed, superseded, or long-lived responsibility evidence.
- **Books** are sellable knowledge products and authority artifacts, not substitutes for current system or commercial evidence.

Journal entries may link to these destinations, but they do not silently redefine their current status. When an event changes a canonical product fact, the authoritative product document or later public system/product page must also be updated.

## Evidence Is Claim-Specific

Evidence must be attached to the claim it actually supports. More evidence volume does not repair a mismatch between evidence and claim.

| Evidence | Supports | Does not by itself support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Working software or technical artifact | The behavior or artifact exists and can be inspected in a stated context. | External demand, operational maturity, or customer value. |
| Documented current development of Alex's own software with AI-agent participation | The Wired Geese model is practiced; agents are normal working participants under Alex's control; TeqFW and ADSM are exercised across the stated software types. | Autonomous development, exclusive AI authorship, superior productivity or quality, external usability, market demand, or commercial viability. |
| Alex's repeated use | Practical internal utility and continued operation for Alex's workflow. | Independent demand, general usability, or willingness to pay. |
| Close or experimental user installation | Operation outside Alex's own environment under stated conditions. | Repeatable delivery, broad trust acceptance, or commercial validation. |
| External user feedback | A real user's observation, problem, or perceived value. | Willingness to pay, retention, or general market fit. |
| Paid pilot or paid setup | At least one payer accepted a bounded experiment or outcome. | Repeatability, customer success, retention, or scalable economics. |
| Paid integration | A customer paid for a bounded integration result. | A standardized offer or independent product. |
| Repeat delivery | The problem and delivery occurred more than once. | Reuse, consistent margin, or broad market size unless measured. |
| Reuse across customers | A capability reduced duplicated work across distinct deliveries. | A mature product, recurring value, or subscription readiness. |
| Measurable customer outcome | A stated outcome occurred for the measured customer and context. | General causality or the same result for other customers. |
| Recurring use | Users repeatedly receive value over time. | Willingness to pay or economically viable service delivery. |
| Recurring revenue | A payer repeatedly pays for continuing value. | Durable retention, scale, or profitability without sufficient duration and economics. |
| Published book or substantial writing | Authorship, depth of explanation, and a knowledge product exists. | Delivery of software or customer results. |
| Long-lived historical production responsibility | Architecture, integration, deployment, operations, maintenance, and accountability over time. | Modern agentic-AI product delivery or current market validation. |
| Public chronology | Work and learning were documented over time. | Truth of every claim without supporting artifacts or observations. |

Evidence can support several claims when each inference is explicit and bounded. A public statement must not silently climb from `used once` to `reliable`, from `paid once` to `validated`, or from `recurring payment` to `scalable SaaS`.

## Own-Software Development Evidence

Alex's current own-software development is first-class internal evidence for the Wired Geese thesis. Applications, frameworks, libraries and packages, infrastructure components, experimental and product systems, and wiredgeese.com may collectively show that the same human-directed agent model is used across different software roles rather than only described as an aspiration.

The recursive case of wiredgeese.com supports the bounded claim that the site is developed through the model it explains. Evidence of agent participation must still preserve Alex's intent, authorization, verification, acceptance, and accountability. It must not imply that every task is delegated or that autonomous agents operate the software.

This evidence category applies to current development unless a system is explicitly documented otherwise. It does not retroactively classify historical software as agent-developed. Historical work requires its own contemporaneous evidence and otherwise supports only the narrower responsibility claims appropriate to it.

## Current Evidence Baseline

The accepted current baseline is limited:

- Alex applies human-directed development with AI agents across the current own software estate defined in `overview.md`, including wiredgeese.com itself, unless a particular system is explicitly documented otherwise.
- Alex uses software developed along the Alarisa direction and PDE capabilities in his own digital life.
- Selected capabilities have been installed for close or experimental users.
- Current TeqFW, PDE, Alarisa-related, TeqCMS, package, infrastructure, and site work provides working evidence that TeqFW, ADSM, and AI-agent participation are exercised in real development. wiredgeese.com is part of this evidence because its maintained context and software are developed through the model it describes.
- The ADSM book is published through Leanpub in Russian and English; the browser book exists in print.
- Older engineering work provides real evidence of long-term system responsibility. Santegra is a substantial Magento-based commerce system with integrations, MLM/distributor management, balances, points, and recurring bonus calculations; it was developed over roughly two years, has operated in production since 2018, and remains supported.

These facts establish current practice and bounded internal engineering evidence. They do not establish autonomous development, superior productivity or quality, broad external demand for PDE, repeatable MCP integration delivery, mature support, production-grade security, successful external cognitive-context transfer, external usability across the estate, recurring product value, or economic sustainability. Those claims require their own external, commercial, comparative, or longitudinal evidence.

## Commercial Evidence Ladder

Commercial development should seek stronger evidence in this approximate order without treating it as a guaranteed funnel:

`working system -> external use -> paid use -> repeat delivery -> reuse -> measurable customer result -> recurring use -> recurring revenue`

Each step answers a different uncertainty. Skipping a public claim ahead of the evidence is prohibited. A paid setup shows willingness to pay for that setup; it does not prove retention. Reuse shows technical or delivery leverage; it does not prove a standalone product. Recurring use suggests continuing value; it does not justify subscription until payer behavior and delivery economics also support it.

## Wired Geese Proof Responsibilities

The evidence stream should gradually test both engineering viability and economic viability:

- the human-plus-agents model is operational rather than rhetorical;
- Alex can direct and accept increasingly ambitious work;
- TeqFW and ADSM support real software evolution;
- working systems can reach external users;
- bounded commercial experiments produce willingness to pay;
- paid work creates reusable capability and stronger owned systems;
- repeated patterns can become standardized products;
- continuing value can eventually support recurring revenue;
- the overall practice can become economically sustainable.

Alarisa is a major engineering proof direction inside this broader test. wiredgeese.com is the surface that makes both the engineering and economic hypotheses visible. Neither is presently proven complete.

## Disclosure And Trust Boundaries

Evidence must be public only when disclosure is lawful, contractually allowed, safe, and proportionate. Customer identity, credentials, private messages, personal data, infrastructure details, and confidential outcomes must not be exposed merely to strengthen a narrative.

An anonymized or aggregated event may be used when it still supports a precise claim. When confidentiality prevents strong public evidence, the site must narrow the claim instead of inventing detail. `cannot disclose` is not equivalent to public proof.

The site must distinguish Alex's technology and decisions from third-party platforms, infrastructure, and agent environments. Use of ChatGPT, Codex, Claude, Telegram, MCP, or another system does not transfer ownership of those systems or imply endorsement, partnership, or guarantees.

## Editorial Honesty Rules

- Publish failures, reversals, rejected assumptions, and discontinued work when they materially affect the public hypothesis.
- State current status at publication time and preserve enough chronology to explain later changes.
- Do not use activity volume, agent count, commits, messages, visits, or clicks as a proxy for customer value or revenue.
- Do not present synthetic demonstrations, internal use, friends-and-family use, or unpaid experiments as independent commercial validation.
- Do not generalize one customer's result without evidence that the relevant conditions repeat.
- Do not imply security, privacy, uptime, support, scale, or compliance beyond an explicit and evidenced boundary.
- Do not describe future plans as delivered capability.
- Correct material public errors or outdated status with a visible update rather than silently preserving a misleading story.

## Distribution Responsibilities

- **Home** shows recent meaningful activity with enough context to expose current work and route into the Journal.
- **Journal index** makes recent events, ongoing threads, statuses, and relevant categories discoverable without requiring a chronological trawl.
- **System, product, and offer pages** link to events that support their specific current claims.
- **Alarisa** links to its active proof threads, including failures and open questions.
- **How the work is made** links to events that show accountable human decisions and real agent participation.
- **Historical surfaces** retain useful context but do not displace recent evidence from first-time attention.

Exact public labels, URLs, feeds, categories, metadata, visual timelines, and event schemas remain later information-design and implementation decisions.

## Open Questions

- Which public label best communicates the relationship between Journal and Events in English, Russian, and Spanish?
- What minimum evidence can be disclosed from paid work without exposing customer confidentiality?
- Which current development threads should seed the first cumulative chronology?
- How should corrections and status changes appear publicly?
- Which measures can be collected ethically and consistently enough to support product and economic claims?
- When does the evidence volume justify filters, thread pages, or a dedicated visual timeline?

These questions do not weaken the first-class requirement: current activity and accumulating evidence must remain prominent even before the final presentation model is selected. Prominence requires direct discovery and meaningful selection, not a publishing quota or equal implementation effort with current commercial conversion.

## Site Purpose

`wiredgeese.com` is a commercial system for Alex Gusev's independent software-making practice.

Its first-class product requirement is to create sustainable revenue from:

- original software products and working capabilities;
- bounded deployment, installation, and integration offers built on those capabilities;
- paid customization and specialized engineering around reusable technology;
- related engineering work for which Alex's existing product base creates a material advantage.

Traffic, reputation, technical originality, writing, experience, and projects are valuable to the site only when they also help suitable visitors understand the work, trust the evidence, and enter economically useful relationships. Revenue is an intended product outcome, not an accidental consequence of professional visibility.

## Commercial Identity

The primary identity is:

`creator and developer of modern web applications for a world in which people, software services, and AI agents coexist and interact`

Alex operates as an independent software maker and engineering product creator. Web Platform and JavaScript technologies are deliberate foundations of this direction, not the commercial product itself and not a reason to reduce the site to a generic JavaScript-developer portfolio.

The software, technologies, and products are created and directed by one accountable human maker, Alex Gusev, working closely with AI and LLM agents as active production participants. Alex remains responsible for product direction and accepted engineering decisions. Agents accelerate software creation but are not employees, do not remove human responsibility, and do not imply the delivery capacity or continuity of a larger organization.

For suitable buyers, this maker-led model can support trust through direct access to the creator, coherent architectural ownership, a short path from feedback to change, and the ability to customize underlying technology. These are conditional positioning strengths, not universal advantages or substitutes for evidence about the product itself.

The commercial progression for a bounded customer solution is:

`useful capability -> independently valuable product -> concrete paid offer -> deployment or integration -> revenue and feedback -> reusable improvement`

Alex's engineering experience remains visible and important. Its commercial role is to prove that the products can be built, maintained, deployed, integrated, and extended. It is also a secondary capability for work adjacent to those products. The site must not make generic access to Alex as an individual developer its principal commodity.

## Product-Led Positioning

The site should lead with things that exist, work, or can be delivered, then explain the technology and experience behind them.

The internal development lineage is:

1. Tequila Framework (`TeqFW`), Agent-Driven Software Management (`ADSM`), and active AI-agent practice form a broader software-production system;
2. Alarisa is the principal long-term R&D project built with that system: an active experimental working system and integration environment, not a current commercial product;
3. Personal Digital Embassy (`PDE`) is an independently useful infrastructure subsystem created as part of Alarisa R&D;
4. Telegram, Shared Files, the World Map, general memory, and other work may yield capabilities, but a capability becomes a product only when it solves a bounded payer need and can be offered, delivered, and supported independently.

The current customer-facing entry path is:

1. a concrete paid experimental Telegram integration offer that a buyer can understand without learning Alarisa or PDE internals;
2. paid deployment, integration, and customization around PDE-based or other independently useful products;
3. related engineering in which TeqFW, ADSM, Alarisa, PDE, and AI-agent practice create reusable leverage;
4. engineering experience, historical systems, public work, and writing as evidence.

Commercial readiness and strategic importance are different dimensions. Alarisa remains the main long-term R&D direction while independently useful results of that work may reach buyers first.

Development lineage and the commercial acquisition path are related but not identical. A buyer may enter directly through a concrete Telegram problem and offer; PDE or Alarisa need appear only when they help the decision. The buyer does not adopt Alarisa merely because a product originated in its R&D.

The product and status distinctions are defined in `product-system.md`. The revenue model is defined in `commercial-strategy.md`. Concrete buyer-facing offer boundaries are defined under `offers/`.

## Buyer Understanding Requirement

A first-time visitor should be able to answer quickly:

1. What does Wired Geese build?
2. What can I buy now?
3. What concrete problem can it solve for me?
4. Why is this more than generic AI automation?
5. Is its experimental status, trust boundary, and delivery model suitable for me?
6. Who is accountable for the product, and what technology and experience support it?
7. What should I do next if I want it?

Internal names such as PDE Runtime, Desk architecture, TDLib, MCP implementation details, TeqFW, and ADSM must not be prerequisites for understanding buyer value.

## Public Surface Roles

The durable information-architecture principle is:

`preservation does not imply navigational prominence`

The future site supports two semantic public layers. They need not correspond one-to-one with runtime areas, routes, or pages.

The **primary commercial surface** helps a new visitor understand what Wired Geese builds now, what can be bought now, which problem an available product solves, whether its boundaries fit, who and what stand behind it, and the next commercial action. It prioritizes:

- active products and offers;
- current technology or product direction where it helps the decision;
- deployment, integration, and customization capability;
- trust, accountable maker identity, and honest maturity boundaries;
- a clear commercial next step.

The **secondary knowledge and archive surface** preserves and exposes:

- Projects, Library, Journal, Books, and older project pages;
- historical systems and experiments;
- technical materials, research, and writing;
- search-bearing content, external references, and historical proof.

This accumulated material acts as commercial support infrastructure through search acquisition, evidence, intellectual context, technical proof, history, and trust formation. It remains part of the site without defining the main product hierarchy.

The future site must also let visitors distinguish at least:

- things available to buy or use now;
- Alarisa as the strategically central long-term R&D project, its future product ambition, and the status of work emerging from it;
- active products, technologies, and working capabilities;
- deployment, integration, and customization capability;
- research and methodology;
- proof and historical responsibility;
- books, library material, and journal writing;
- the contact or commercial next step.

Primary navigation serves the current business and technological direction rather than reproducing the historical personal-site taxonomy. Projects, Library, Journal, Books, and comparable accumulated material use secondary discovery. Contact is a distinct commercial action and continuity route rather than a primary content category.

Secondary status must not orphan retained content. The approved information architecture uses a `Resources` hub, footer navigation, contextual links, related-content links, preserved inbound routes, and deliberate redirects.

The concrete target hierarchy, routes, and page roles are defined in `information-architecture.md`. Final visual composition and public copy remain separate design work.

## Provisional Early Adopter Fit

The provisional early adopter profile favors buyers whose needs and technological attitudes fit experimental, maker-led software. They may be technically thoughtful individuals, independent professionals, makers, founders, or small teams who:

- already use ChatGPT or other AI and LLM systems as practical working tools;
- want those systems to interact with more of their real digital environment;
- value control over data, credentials, infrastructure, and explicit trust boundaries;
- accept useful experimental software that is not yet a polished mass-market SaaS;
- value direct access to the person building and adapting the system;
- can evaluate honest trade-offs, self-hosting options, and customization needs.

This is a behavioral and psychographic fit, not a demographic category. Customers need not be programmers or operate servers themselves; Alex may provide deployment where an explicit agreement makes that suitable.

Finding people whose needs and technological attitudes resemble Alex's is a useful early product-discovery heuristic because PDE arose partly from needs mainstream services did not meet in the desired form. It is not a permanent market definition. Evidence from paying external customers must refine or replace it.

## Evidence And Honesty

Claims must be attached to inspectable evidence wherever practical. The site must distinguish:

- available now from planned or speculative;
- working but experimental from production-mature;
- repeatable capability from one historical achievement;
- productized outcomes from open-ended engineering;
- the strategic importance of a system from the present commercial readiness of each component;
- technology owned by Alex from third-party infrastructure.

The site must not manufacture a history of many successful independent product businesses, customer results, testimonials, maturity, security guarantees, or market validation that do not exist.

Alarisa and PDE capabilities are used in Alex's own digital life, and selected capabilities have been installed for close or experimental users. This is evidence of practical usefulness and operation. It is not evidence of market demand, mature support, production-grade security, repeatable external deployment, or commercial validation. External paying customers remain the required validation evidence.

## Historical And Search Preservation

The commercial hierarchy may change radically without casually destroying accumulated search and reference value.

Future implementation must:

- inventory valuable existing URLs and search-bearing content before restructuring;
- prefer keeping useful URLs valid;
- keep useful articles, books, project pages, technical materials, and historical content accessible even when their hierarchy changes;
- use deliberate redirects when a URL must change;
- preserve incoming links and indexed material unless a reviewed reason justifies removal;
- keep retained content meaningfully discoverable through at least one approved secondary or contextual path rather than merely leaving files on the server;
- change commercial emphasis without erasing legitimate history.

Preservation does not require obsolete offers or historical sections to remain promoted, described as current, or represented in primary navigation. Search value is an asset, not authority over product strategy.

## Target State And Legacy Implementation

This context defines the accepted target state. The current site may still contain engineer-centric service copy, the discontinued GitHub Flows experiment, old prices, and associated landing-page mechanics. Those are legacy implementation drift, not requirements to preserve or rebuild.

Implementation changes require a later, separately approved task.

## Communication Principles

Public communication should be calm, specific, technically credible, commercially clear, honest about experimental boundaries, and ambitious about direction.

It must avoid:

- generic `AI expert`, commodity developer, or vague digital-transformation positioning;
- aggressive sales language, invented urgency, and exaggerated productivity claims;
- generic corporate or artificial enterprise language that hides the single-maker model;
- selling abstract architecture before buyer outcomes;
- presenting speculative capabilities or experimental infrastructure as mature products;
- defensive use of Alex's age or grievances about market comparisons;
- leading with legacy Magento/PHP work or a long autobiography;
- implying legal, privacy, security, or availability guarantees that have not been established.

For the provisional early adopter audience, technical transparency, explicit trade-offs, controlled deployment, direct maker involvement, and optional architectural depth may support a trust decision. Buyer outcomes still come first; Alex's identity and production model support product value rather than replace it.

## Product Review Rule

Every substantial future site revision must verify that:

- a buyer can identify at least one concrete purchasable outcome;
- Alarisa remains visible as the principal long-term R&D project without being presented as something a buyer can purchase now;
- capabilities, independently deliverable products, and their concrete commercial offers remain distinguishable;
- original technology leads to a credible delivery and customization path;
- engineering expertise supports the products rather than replacing them;
- current, experimental, historical, and future items are visibly distinct;
- a commercial next step is explicit and proportionate;
- useful public and search-bearing material is preserved deliberately;
- primary navigation serves current commercial goals while retained knowledge remains meaningfully reachable through secondary paths;
- Alex remains the accountable human maker and AI agents remain visible production participants without exaggerated capacity claims;
- practical use is not presented as commercial validation;
- early adopter communication describes needs and attitudes rather than demographic or unnecessary technical gates.
